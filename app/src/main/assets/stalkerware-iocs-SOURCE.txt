stalkerware-iocs.json is derived from Echap's stalkerware-indicators:

  https://github.com/Te-k/stalkerware-indicators
  ioc.yaml (stalkerware) and watchware.yaml (watchware)
  Licence: CC-BY 4.0 (attribution required; compatible with Bulwark's GPLv3)
  Snapshot taken: 2026-09-14

Regenerate with, from the repo root:

  python tools/build-stalkerware-iocs.py ioc.yaml watchware.yaml

Field names are shortened to keep the asset small; nothing is altered:
  n = name          (what the tool calls itself)
  t = type          ("stalkerware" | "watchware")
  p = packages      (Android package names)
  c = certificates  (signing certificate SHA-1, lower-case hex)

Upstream carries more than this: C2 domains, distribution URLs, websites, iOS
bundle ids, and two fuzzy certificate matches. Bulwark ships none of them, and
tools/build-stalkerware-iocs.py says why for each. Bytes we would never read
are bytes a user carries for nothing.

Counts at this snapshot, printed by the script:
  158 of 174 upstream entries are matchable by package name or certificate
  16 carry neither, so nothing here can recognise them
  641 distinct packages, 484 distinct certificates

That 16 is not a rounding error, it is coverage Bulwark does not have, and the
screen has to say so. "Nothing found" is never proof that nothing is there.

**Stalkerware and watchware are kept apart deliberately.** Upstream separates
them by file and so does this. Watchware is parental-control and employee-
monitoring software: often installed openly, often by someone entitled to do
it. Telling a person that is stalkerware is its own harm, and so is the
reverse.

This is years of careful work by people who do it for the people it protects.
Credit it in the app, not only here - and cite the Coalition Against
Stalkerware rather than paraphrasing advice we are not qualified to give.
